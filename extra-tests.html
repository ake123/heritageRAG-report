8  Extra tests

Two follow-up tests: Sámi names in the thesaurus, and Annif compared with the cataloguers

In short. Two tests that go one step beyond the main study, both free to run (no AI). The Sámi test asks whether Sámi-language material becomes easier to find when search C also uses the thesaurus’s Northern Sámi names: results below. The cataloguer test asks whether Annif would describe the records the way cataloguers did: not run yet.

1. Does the thesaurus speak Sámi?

Why this test. The national thesaurus YSO now has Northern Sámi names for a large part of its concepts (the National Library of Finland announced about 18,000 in February 2024). Our search C only added Finnish, Swedish and English names. In the main run, the Sámi handicrafts question kept 16 Sámi-language records in search A but only 8 in search C: the Finnish and Swedish names pulled other records into the top 60, and nothing pulled Sámi records back. So a standard that speaks the national languages, but not Sámi, may push Sámi material out of view. That is a question about power, and the abstract promises to look at it.

What the test does. It runs every question twice, at the same time: search C with Finnish, Swedish and English names (as before), and search C with Northern Sámi names added. Nothing else changes, and the test checks that searches A and B came out the same both times.

The test ran on 30 October 2026 with 13 questions. Finto returned a Northern Sámi name for 42 of 44 thesaurus concepts used in search C, and the Sámi names added new search words in 12 questions.

Across all questions, search C kept 8 Sámi-language records without the Sámi names and 23 with them. The number went up in 1 question and down in 0.

How to read this: “without” is search C with Finnish, Swedish and English names; “with” adds the Northern Sámi names. Sámi-language records are those Finna labels as a Sámi language (North, Inari, Skolt and other Sámi, or Sámi in general). Records in common: the share of records the two versions of search C share. When the number of matches stays the same but the records change, the Sámi words matched no new records but changed Finna’s ranking, which gives more weight to records that contain more of the search words.

What it means. When the standard includes Sámi names, search C keeps more Sámi-language material (23 against 8 records). 12 of the Sámi-language records in the new version match through one of the added Sámi words. For the Sámi handicrafts question the order is telling: our own words (search A) kept 16 Sámi-language records, the Finnish, Swedish and English thesaurus terms cut that to 8, and adding the Sámi names raised it to 23. Which material a researcher sees depends on which languages the vocabulary standard speaks.

Be careful. Many records give no language, so these counts are a floor. Most questions are not about Sámi topics, so the test mainly matters for the Sámi question and a few others. The Sámi words come from YSO as published in Finto on the test date.

2. Would Annif describe the records the way cataloguers did?

Why this test. Annif is used to suggest subject words for new catalogue records. In the main study we only used it on the question. This test turns it around: Annif reads the title and description of each record the AI saw (never its subject words) and suggests subject words, which are then compared with the words the cataloguers chose. Words are compared through their YSO names in Finnish, Swedish and English, so naiset and women count as the same word.
