6  Compare the searches

Which institutions, languages, material types, decades and subjects each search shows, question by question

In short. For every question, the charts below compare the records kept by the three searches: where they come from, what language they are in, what kind of material they are, which decade they date from, and which subject words describe them. Each tab starts with a one-line note on what changed between search A and search C.

WarningCheckers: please stop here

These charts show which search found records from which institution. If you are judging records or sentences for the study, please come back after you have finished.

How to read the charts

  • Each group of three bars is one category (for example one museum). The bars show what share of each search’s kept records falls in that category: A: own words B: + Annif C: + YSO terms
  • Shares, not counts, so that a search with 11 records and one with 57 can be compared. The small number at the end of each bar is the actual number of records.
  • Purple, bold labels are categories that search A did not show at all: they appear only through Annif’s or the thesaurus’s words.
  • Grey labels are categories that search A showed but search C does not: the wider search pushed them out of its top 60.
  • Each chart shows the ten largest categories. (not given) means the record has no value for that field. In the language charts it is left out and reported in the note instead, because most records (photographs and objects) have no language.
